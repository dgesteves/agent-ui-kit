Human-in-the-loop approval with a command or argument preview, a risk level when you give one (without `risk`, no badge rather than a guess), and deny with feedback. Y and N work while focus is in the card; ⌘ or Ctrl + Enter approves, optionally page-wide. `critical` actions need a second, confirming press.

Each pending approval sends one decision: a double click, or Y then N, is ignored until `status` changes, the handler's promise settles or the handler throws. `ToolApprovalCard` binds the card to a tool part and to `addToolApprovalResponse`, including the denial reason, and renders nothing for calls that need no approval.

Decisions that a `toolApproval` rule makes on its own (`approval.isAutomatic`) never prompt or take focus, and read "Auto-approved" or "Blocked by policy" rather than as a person's decision. A custom `preview` is your content: with `styles.css`, the kit's rules don't reach into it.

**Once, for the session or always.** `decisions` sets the choices: `allow-once`, `allow-session`, `allow-always`, `deny-once` and `deny-always`. The default is Approve and Deny, once. A lasting choice says what it will cover: the tool, and argument patterns the person can edit. For a command, the pattern starts at its program and subcommand (`npm test*`); for other calls, it starts at each argument as it is. "Any arguments" covers every call of the tool. `onDecide` receives `{ decision, reason, input, args }`; when you pass it, `onApprove` and `onDeny` are not called. With a `policy` from `useApprovalPolicy`, `ToolApprovalCard` offers every choice, records the rule a person makes, and answers a call a rule already covers without showing a card. Once answered, that call reads "Allowed by your rule" and shows the rule.

**Edit the arguments, then approve.** With `editable` (on `ApprovalCard`) or `onEditInput` (on `ToolApprovalCard`), "Edit arguments" opens the arguments for editing. An object of strings, numbers and booleans gets a form; any other input gets JSON, which must parse before anything can be approved. The edited input goes out with the approval. `ToolApprovalCard` offers editing only with `onEditInput`, the function that applies the edit, so an edit is never dropped on its way to the agent. With AI SDK 7, `onEditInput={(id, input) => setMessages((m) => setToolInput(m, id, input))}` puts the edited input in the messages; the server runs the call with it after checking it against the tool's schema and its `toolApproval` again. With `useAgUiAgent`, pass its `editInput`, and the approval's resume payload carries `input`.

**Approve all.** `ToolApprovalBatch` answers every approval a run is waiting on at once, such as parallel calls; with a critical one among them it asks for a second press. `AgentMessage` shows it above its cards when two or more approvals are waiting.
